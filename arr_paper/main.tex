% ABSTRACT
% ============================================================================
\begin{abstract}
Low-rank adaptation (LoRA) can fail silently, and the cheapest proposed checks read the adapter's own weights, operationalised as a threshold on spectral energy (SE), the concentration of the update's singular-value spectrum. We test such checks where they matter most: low-resource, morphologically complex languages without full evaluation suites. Fine-tuning Gemma-2-9B with QLoRA on Kyrgyz, Kazakh and Uzbek, with a Llama-3-8B replication, we find: \textbf{(1)} an SE threshold misses learning-rate-induced collapse; runs with near-identical, sub-threshold spectra span the full range from catastrophic collapse to healthy behaviour. A controlled decomposition traces the collapse to learning rate, rules out 4-bit quantization, and shows the apparent benefit of higher rank to be an artefact of the $\alpha{=}2r$ convention. \textbf{(2)} Frobenius norm growth separates regimes within a language category but not across. \textbf{(3)} The final adapter is directionally underdetermined: across seeds, direct LoRA lands in near-orthogonal directions, and the collapse-vs-healthy gap lies inside this seed-noise band ($2$--$8\times$ at three seeds), leaving no leverage for scale-invariant functions of the final adapter. The signal is in scale, and in its trajectory: the early growth rate of $\|B\|_F$, read off the first $10\%$ of training, separates all 42 logged runs on both architectures without SVD or held-out data, and predicted four further runs at unseen rank, learning-rate and language settings before they were evaluated. \textbf{(4)} Related-language warm-start (Kazakh$\rightarrow$Kyrgyz) halves cross-lingual forgetting on both architectures; a cross-family warm-start does not. We release all code, SVD logs and per-run evaluation reports.\footnote{\label{fn:repo}{\small\url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/Spectral-Collapse-Turkic-Conference-6DE4}}}
\end{abstract}

% 1. INTRODUCTION

\section{Introduction}

A fine-tuning run can fail silently, and the cheapest way to notice is supposed to be the adapter's own weights---no evaluation suite, no held-out data, just a number read off the matrices. In the setting where that shortcut is needed most, it does not work: the standard diagnostic misses catastrophic failure, and no direction-based statistic of the final weights does better, and the final norm has no threshold that transfers. The signal is in the trajectory, not its endpoint.

Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA; \citealp{hu2022lora}) fine-tunes a large language model by learning an additive update $\Delta W = BA$ to each frozen weight matrix, where $A$ and $B$ are trainable factors of rank $r \ll \min(d,k)$ and a scaling hyperparameter $\alpha$ sets the update's magnitude; it is the standard tool for compute-constrained fine-tuning. \citet{biderman2024lora} analysed LoRA through the singular-value spectrum of the weight update, on English at scale, and tied its weaker in-domain learning to the low rank of the update. The natural scalar reading of that analysis, and the one implemented in our monitoring tooling, is \emph{spectral energy} $\text{SE} = \sigma_1 / \sum_i \sigma_i$ with a collapse flag when the update concentrates into one direction, $\text{SE} > 0.7$. We know of no published calibration of that cutoff against functional outcomes; it is the operational rule we test.

Whether such diagnostics transfer to low-resource settings is where the answer matters most. Practitioners adapting LLMs to underrepresented languages rarely have evaluation suites for every checkpoint, so a health signal read off the weights would be the natural substitute; yet adapters there must make larger perturbations, and whether thresholds tuned on English survive has not been tested. Our research question: \emph{can a statistic of the final adapter's weights be trusted to detect fine-tuning failure in this regime?} The answer is negative, and it carries a concrete consequence: do not gate training health on a spectral-energy threshold, watch the trajectory instead, and prefer a related-language warm-start where one exists (\Cref{sec:trajectory_probe}).

We test three hypotheses on Gemma-2-9B and replicate the central claims on Llama-3-8B, using Kyrgyz, Kazakh, and Uzbek as a stress test ($3.2\times$ subword fragmentation under standard tokenizers, severe pretraining underrepresentation). \textbf{H1} (SE threshold): SE${>}0.7$ predicts functional degradation. \textbf{H2} (transfer): related-language warm-start preserves source-language knowledge at minimal Frobenius perturbation. \textbf{H3} (directional discriminator): some scalar geometric function of the final adapter separates collapse from healthy training at the same rank.

\textbf{Contributions.} \textbf{(C1)} A controlled decomposition (E5/E5b/E5c plus an absolute-$\alpha$ control C5) \textbf{falsifies the SE-threshold hypothesis} for low-resource quantized LoRA: four $r{=}64$ runs at sub-threshold SE span the full functional range, a BF16 control rules out quantization, and C5 shows the rank advantage of E5c is the $\alpha{=}2r$ convention amplifying absolute $\alpha$, not rank (\Cref{sec:spectral,sec:spectral-collapse-rank-lr}). \textbf{(C2)} Frobenius growth is a language-dependent diagnostic that fails across language categories (\Cref{sec:frobenius}). \textbf{(C3)} We \textbf{falsify H3 and measure an underdetermination bound} (\Cref{sec:underdetermination}): direct LoRA is directionally non-canonical across seeds (cosine $0.01$--$0.04$, $0/294$ modules above $0.5$), the collapse-vs-healthy gap sits inside that band at $n{=}3$, and the same inequality holds on Llama-3. \textbf{(C4)} A \textbf{trajectory probe}, the early growth rate of $\|B\|_F$, separates every collapsed run from every healthy one on both architectures at a tenth of the training budget (\Cref{sec:trajectory_probe}). \textbf{(C5)} Related-language warm-start \textbf{preserves source-language knowledge}: KZ$\rightarrow$KY transfer halves cross-lingual KZ PPL on both architectures and under independent reseeding, while a cross-family (EN$\rightarrow$KY) control does not; transfer pins the adapter to its source (\Cref{sec:transfer,sec:directional}). \textbf{(C6)} A Llama-3-8B replication of five configurations reproduces every finding (\Cref{app:llama3}); we release all training, evaluation and SVD-monitoring code.

% 2. RELATED WORK

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Weight-based health diagnostics.} A fine-tuning run can fail in ways that only a full evaluation suite reveals---but running one on every checkpoint is expensive, and for many languages the suites do not exist. This motivated a line of work reading training health directly off the weights, which is cheap and needs no task data. \citet{martin2021implicit} showed that the singular-value spectra of well-trained matrices carry a signature of implicit self-regularisation, establishing spectra as a proxy for training quality. In the LoRA setting, \citet{biderman2024lora} analysed the singular-value spectrum of the learned update, measuring its rank as the number of singular values needed to explain $90\%$ of the variance, and found LoRA's updates far lower-rank than full fine-tuning's. The operational rule that follows in practice is a scalar concentration statistic, \emph{spectral energy} $\text{SE} = \sigma_1/\sum_i \sigma_i$, with collapse flagged above a cutoff ($0.7$ in our tooling) once the update concentrates into a single direction; neither the statistic nor the cutoff has, to our knowledge, been calibrated against functional outcomes. \citet{jang2024lora} analysed LoRA's optimisation trajectory in the NTK regime. Both lines of work are established on English at large scale; whether a spectral threshold survives elsewhere is what we test.

\textbf{LoRA and its variants.} LoRA \cite{hu2022lora} learns $\Delta W = BA$ at rank $r$; QLoRA \cite{dettmers2023qlora} adds a 4-bit quantised base. Variants change where the adapter starts or how it moves: rank allocation \cite{zhang2023adalora}, DoRA \citep{liu2024dora}, PiSSA \citep{meng2024pissa}, LoRA+ \citep{hayou2024loraplus}. That fine-tuning succeeds at low rank at all is consistent with its low intrinsic dimension \cite{aghajanyan2021intrinsic}, a fact we return to in \Cref{sec:underdetermination}: a low-dimensional solution space says nothing about \emph{which} subspace a run selects.

\textbf{Forgetting under adaptation.} Adapting a model to a new language degrades the old ones \cite{mccloskey1989catastrophic,kirkpatrick2017overcoming}, and low-rank constraints do not prevent it \cite{luo2023forget,kotha2024understanding,kumar2022finetuning}; severity grows with distance from the pretraining distribution \cite{csaki2024sambalingo}. Our sequential transfer construction is closest in spirit to MAD-X \cite{pfeiffer2020madx}, but warm-starts a single adapter rather than composing parallel language adapters.

\textbf{Turkic NLP.} Standardised evaluation is recent: TUMLU \cite{isbarov2024tumlu} covers multi-task understanding across Turkic languages, and tokenizer mismatch for agglutinative morphology is quantified across model families \cite{rust2021good,ahia2023all}---the fragmentation that makes these languages a stress test for adaptation.

\textbf{This work.} Weight-based diagnostics are validated where evaluation is cheap and applied where it is not; we test whether that transfer is warranted and find that it is not.

\section{Experimental Setup}

\subsection{Base Model}

We use Gemma-2-9B \cite{team2024gemma}, a 9.24B-parameter decoder-only transformer with 42 layers, quantized to 4-bit NF4 with double quantization \cite{dettmers2023qlora} and bfloat16 compute. Gemma-2-9B has baseline knowledge of Turkic languages from pretraining, so we isolate the dynamics of LoRA \emph{adaptation} from primary language acquisition: the functional collapse at $r{=}64$ is a pathology of adaptation, not an inability to represent Turkic tokens.

\subsection{Training Data}
\label{sec:data}

We compile monolingual corpora of ${\sim}150$~MB per target language: \textbf{Kazakh (KZ)}, 61,879 records from Kazakh Wikipedia and the sozkz-corpus (14.1M tokens); \textbf{Kyrgyz (KY)}, 17,360 records from a curated local corpus of literature, history and encyclopedic text (4.4M tokens);\footnote{Due to licensing restrictions, the Kyrgyz corpus cannot be redistributed; statistics and preprocessing are in \Cref{app:configs}.} and \textbf{Uzbek (UZ)}, 21,242 records from uz-books, Uzbek Wikipedia and FineWeb-2, Cyrillic script only (5.4M tokens), so results may not generalise to Latin-script Uzbek. An \textbf{English (EN)} control corpus of 52,268 English Wikipedia records (155~MB, 12.7M tokens) matches the Turkic corpora in size. Texts are chunked into 200--5,000-character segments with a 10\% held-out split. Corpora are byte-matched, but the training script truncates every record to 256 tokens, so the tokens a run sees depend on record length: Kyrgyz and Uzbek records are almost all 5,000-character chunks (median $4{,}962$ and $4{,}955$ characters), cut to 256 tokens each ($17{,}360 \times 256 = 4.4$M), whereas Kazakh records are shorter (median $825$ characters) and mostly fit, giving 14.1M; fragmentation is nearly identical across the three (\Cref{sec:tokenization}). Each language therefore trains on a different fraction of its corpus, roughly a tenth of the Kyrgyz and Uzbek text. This is a confound for direct E1 vs.\ E3 comparisons (Limitation L5); the reduced-Kazakh control E1b (${\sim}1.5$M tokens, $3\times$ \emph{smaller} than the KY corpus) separates data volume from pretraining coverage (\Cref{sec:crosslingual,app:e1_e1b}).

\subsection{LoRA Configuration}

We apply LoRA to all attention and MLP projection matrices ($q$, $k$, $v$, $o$, gate, up, down — 7 modules per layer, 294 total across 42 Gemma-2 layers; 224 across 32 Llama-3 layers). $\alpha = 2r$ throughout; we use paged AdamW with cosine LR schedule and 5\% warmup; effective batch 16; max seq length 256. The complete configuration grid (all language/rank/LR/epochs/init combinations) is in \Cref{tab:experiments} (\Cref{app:configs}). Every Gemma-2 configuration except the absolute-$\alpha$ control C5 is seed-replicated; the three carrying the underdetermination argument---E3, E5, E5c---are at $n{=}3$ (seeds 42, 123, 7) and the rest at $n{=}2$ (seeds 42, 123; see \Cref{app:seed_variance}). All five Llama-3 configurations are at $n{=}2$.

\subsection{SVD Monitoring Protocol}

We SVD each $\texttt{lora\_B}$ matrix every 100 training steps and log five spectral metrics: spectral energy $\text{SE} = \sigma_1 / \sum_i \sigma_i$ (\Cref{sec:related}), effective rank $\exp(-\sum_i p_i \log p_i)$ with $p_i = \sigma_i / \sum_j \sigma_j$ \citep{roy2007effective}, stable rank $\|W\|_F^2 / \sigma_1^2$, SVD entropy, and Frobenius norm. We monitor $A$ and $B$ separately. The SE-collapse threshold of $0.7$ (\Cref{sec:related}) is the principal diagnostic we test. The pairwise-cosine analysis (\Cref{sec:directional}) is computed on the full $\Delta W = BA$ matrix.

\subsection{Evaluation Protocol}

We evaluate each adapter on three tasks:

\textbf{Perplexity (PPL):} on held-out validation splits (500 samples, 113--128K tokens per language), for every adapter on all three languages: a \emph{cross-lingual perplexity matrix} that measures forgetting directly.

\textbf{Named Entity Recognition (NER):} Using the WikiANN dataset \cite{pan2017cross} with 3-shot prompting on 100 test samples per language. We report two complementary metrics: \textbf{(i)} generation-based F1 micro-averaged across PER/ORG/LOC types (subject to parse-failure noise and $\pm 0.085$ Wilson CI at $n{=}100$); and \textbf{(ii)} log-likelihood span typing accuracy on gold-span boundaries (\Cref{app:ner_loglik}), which is parse-failure immune and is our primary NER number in \Cref{tab:main_results}; F1 is reported alongside for comparability with prior work. \textbf{Caveat for Uzbek:} our UZ corpus is Cyrillic while WikiANN-uz is mostly Latin (entity overlap $2\%$; L9, \Cref{app:overlap}), so UZ NER comparisons measure cross-script transfer.

\textbf{Question Answering (QA):} Using TUMLU \cite{isbarov2024tumlu}, a multiple-choice benchmark for Turkic language understanding, with 5-shot log-likelihood evaluation on 695--794 questions per language.

\section{Results}

An SE-threshold diagnostic assumes the two co-occur; our results show they dissociate. \Cref{tab:main_results} presents the primary metrics.

Target-language PPL tracks training-corpus token count and pretraining coverage (E1 KZ $2.73$, E2 UZ $4.03$, E3 KY $4.78$). The $r{=}64$ high-LR configuration (E5) is the central empirical surprise: target PPL only $5.90$ ($+23\%$ over baseline), but NER F1$=0$ everywhere (100\% parse failures), TUMLU collapses to $23.2\%$ (near 25\% random). Under log-likelihood TypeAcc, however, E5 retains the highest KY type accuracy in the sweep ($59.5\%$), so the collapse is loss of structured-output instruction-following, not erasure of entity knowledge.

\label{sec:spectral-collapse-rank-lr}
\textbf{Rank--LR--epoch--$\alpha$ decomposition (E5 vs.\ E5b vs.\ E5c vs.\ C5).} E5 ($r{=}64$, $\text{lr}{=}5{\times}10^{-4}$, 5 epochs, $\alpha{=}128$) varies four factors at once. E5b matches rank but uses $\text{lr}{=}2{\times}10^{-4}$ (seeds 42, 123): collapse vanishes (F1 $\geq 0.12$, TUMLU $33\%$), so the proximate cause is LR. E5c additionally matches epochs: at matched rank, LR and epochs, $r{=}64$ \emph{appears} to beat the $r{=}16$ baseline (KY PPL $3.86$ vs.\ $4.78$). C5 closes this: holding absolute $\alpha$ at $32$ (as in E3, instead of the $\alpha{=}128$ that $\alpha{=}2r$ dictates at $r{=}64$), $r{=}64$ loses almost all of its apparent advantage: KY PPL $4.46$ vs.\ $4.78$ (E5c: $3.86$), KZ PPL $49.22$ vs.\ $47.58$ (E5c: $93.5$), UZ PPL $57.36$ vs.\ $56.85$, F1 $0.174$ vs.\ $0.157$, TUMLU $33.2\%$ vs.\ $34.7\%$. Cross-lingual PPL, NER and TUMLU are within seed spread or Wilson CI of the $r{=}16$ baseline; target PPL keeps a residual gain of $0.3$, about a third of E5c's, against E3's seed spread of $\pm0.02$. C5 is single-seed on Gemma-2; its two-seed Llama-3 replication shows the same picture ($4.21$/$4.19$ vs.\ $4.32$; \Cref{app:llama3}). Directionally, $\cos$(C5, E3)$=0.155$ exceeds $\cos$(C5, E5c)$=0.072$. Hence: catastrophic collapse $=$ aggressive LR; cross-lingual forgetting growth $=$ training duration; rank at matched absolute $\alpha$ $=$ at most a marginal target-PPL gain with no cross-lingual cost. All four runs have mean SE $< 0.1$ and final max SE $0.06$--$0.24$ while spanning the full functional range; SE never discriminates them.

\subsection{Cross-Lingual Degradation}
\label{sec:crosslingual}

Three patterns drive the rest of the paper (full PPL matrix and heatmap in \Cref{app:cross_ppl_full}). \textbf{(i)} The $r{=}64$ high-LR run (E5) shows catastrophic forgetting: KZ PPL $659.25$, UZ PPL $742.73$ ($14\times$ and $13\times$ the KY baseline's cross-lingual $47.6$ and $56.9$). \textbf{(ii)} KZ$\rightarrow$KY transfer (E6) halves source-language KZ PPL ($23.49$ vs.\ $47.58$ for direct KY training) while target KY PPL is unchanged ($4.73$ vs.\ $4.78$). \textbf{(iii)} The EN control (E8) absorbs comparable Frobenius growth ($33\times$ vs.\ KZ baseline's $38\times$) with KZ PPL of only $5.54$: same magnitude of perturbation produces qualitatively different cross-lingual outcomes depending on pretraining coverage of the target language. We probe the data-volume vs.\ pretraining-coverage decomposition (E1 vs.\ E1b) in \Cref{app:e1_e1b}: E1b at $\sim 1.5$M tokens still attains target KZ PPL $4.17$, below the KY baseline's $4.78$ at $\sim 3\times$ more tokens, so coverage is a genuine confound on top of corpus size.

\subsection{Spectral Energy Is Not a Reliable Collapse Diagnostic}
\label{sec:spectral}

The SE threshold of $0.7$ is never reached in any of our experiments, including those exhibiting severe functional degradation (SE dynamics in \Cref{fig:svd_dynamics}, layer-wise heatmap in \Cref{fig:layer_heatmap}, both in \Cref{app:fig_extras}). The KY baseline ($r{=}16$) peaks at max SE $\sim 0.44$ then decreases to $\sim 0.32$. Both $r{=}64$ configurations---collapsed E5 and healthy E5b---end at final max SE $\sim 0.06$--$0.11$, mean SE $\sim 0.03$, near-identical spectra but with completely different functional outcomes (F1 $0$ vs.\ $0.146$, TUMLU $23\%$ vs.\ $33\%$). SE is a \emph{shape} metric, invariant to rescaling and rank-biased by construction ($\text{SE}\approx 1/r$ at initialisation), so it cannot see the difference (\Cref{app:why_se_fails}). Effective rank tells the same story: both $r{=}64$ runs use most of their capacity yet diverge functionally.

\textbf{Quantization is not the cause (BF16 control, C3).} The full-precision replication of E3, at two seeds, is statistically indistinguishable from 4-bit E3 on the spectral signal (final max SE $0.33\pm0.02$ vs.\ $0.33\pm0.01$, in-training peak $0.49\pm0.02$ vs.\ $0.45\pm0.02$, Frobenius growth $8.5\times$ vs.\ $8.4\times$) and on NER and TUMLU (within the two-seed E3 spread). Target KY PPL is $4.99$ at both BF16 seeds against $4.81\pm0.02$, and cross-lingual KZ/UZ PPL ($46.3\pm2.3$ / $58.3\pm0.7$) overlaps the 4-bit spread ($50.0\pm2.4$ / $55.5\pm1.4$), an order of magnitude below E5's forgetting. The falsification therefore carries to full precision (\Cref{app:bf16}).

\subsection{Frobenius Norm Is Not a Universal Diagnostic}
\label{sec:frobenius}

The growth ratio $\|B\|_F^{\text{final}}/\|B\|_F^{\text{init}}$ captures the scale information SE discards and separates regimes \emph{within} a language category ($1.1\times$ transfer, $8.3$--$8.7\times$ direct KY at either rank, $15.6$--$18.2\times$ for the $r{=}64$ runs that forget severely; final norms in \Cref{app:frob} separate E5 from E5c outright). It fails \emph{across} them: the EN control absorbs $33\times$ growth with minimal forgetting (KZ PPL $5.52\pm0.02$) while the KZ baseline at $38\times$ forgets severely, and E5 versus E5b grow comparably ($15.6\times$ vs.\ $18.2\times$) while only E5 fails. The same perturbation magnitude means different things depending on how well the base covers the target language, so the final norm is not a portable diagnostic---though \Cref{sec:trajectory_probe} shows its \emph{growth rate} is. \Cref{app:frobenius} gives the full table.

\subsection{Cross-Lingual Transfer}
\label{sec:transfer}

Warm-starting the Kyrgyz adapter from the final Kazakh adapter (E6) halves cross-lingual KZ perplexity relative to direct training ($23.5\pm0.1$ vs.\ $50.0\pm2.4$ across two seeds) at $+13\%$ Frobenius growth instead of $8.4\times$, while target KY perplexity is unchanged. The effect is \emph{source-language retention}, not target improvement: a single-seed KY NER gain does not survive reseeding. It reproduces on Llama-3 ($30.4 \to 19.0$) and under fully independent reseeding of both stages ($50.0 \to 26.8$), whereas the cross-family control EN$\rightarrow$KY does not reproduce it ($48.8\pm1.4$, inside the direct-training spread). Related-language transfer is therefore qualitatively different from arbitrary checkpoint reuse; \Cref{app:transfer} gives the full comparison, including the UZ degradation that two-step Kipchak specialisation induces.

\subsection{Directional Geometry of LoRA Solutions}
\label{sec:directional}

Spectral energy and Frobenius growth are both functions of the singular-value spectrum of $\Delta W{=}BA$, and both fail to separate E5 from E5c. The next probe is \emph{direction}: do the two configurations move the weights along the same axis? We measure per-module Frobenius cosine between every pair of final adapters,
\begin{equation}
\cos_m(\Delta W_X, \Delta W_Y) \;=\; \frac{\langle B_X A_X,\; B_Y A_Y\rangle_F}{\|B_X A_X\|_F\,\|B_Y A_Y\|_F},
\label{eq:cosine}
\end{equation}
computed without materializing the $\text{out}\times\text{in}$ product (\Cref{app:cosine_details}). We report the mean and median of $\cos_m$ over the $42\times 7 = 294$ LoRA-equipped projections in Gemma-2-9B.

The 22-pair sweep (\Cref{tab:pairwise_cos}, \Cref{fig:pairwise_cos}) reveals structure that no spectral or Frobenius diagnostic surfaces.

\subsection{Underdetermination: Seed Noise Bounds Scale-Invariant Final-Adapter Diagnostics}
\label{sec:underdetermination}

\Cref{sec:directional} supports a stronger negative claim than ``SE is the wrong shape metric'', with a scope we state carefully. Let $D$ be any \emph{scale-invariant} scalar diagnostic of the final adapter: a function of the direction of $\Delta W{=}BA$ alone, which covers the spectral-shape statistics (SE, effective rank, stable rank, entropy) and cosines to any fixed reference such as $W_0$. Reliable discrimination requires the between-configuration gap $\Delta_D$ to exceed the within-configuration spread $\sigma_D^{\text{seed}}$. At $n{=}3$ (seeds 42, 123, 7) the pairwise cosines, the only geometry such a $D$ can see, give: within-configuration E5c $0.0316{\pm}0.0002$, E5 $0.0221{\pm}0.0003$, E3 $0.0423{\pm}0.0013$ (mean${\pm}$SD over three cross-seed pairs); between-configuration, at each seed, E5 vs.\ E5c $0.0101$ (max $0.0103$) and E5 vs.\ E3 $0.0053$. The \emph{smallest} within-configuration value exceeds the \emph{largest} between-configuration gap for every pair, by $2$--$8\times$, with the seed spread two orders of magnitude below that margin. In this geometry a collapsed and a healthy adapter are no further apart than two seeds of one configuration, so the Lipschitz bound $|D(x)-D(y)| \le L\,d(x,y)$ gives a direction-based $D$ no more room to separate the regimes than it has to separate seeds; every such $D$ we tried fails empirically, including our $W_0$-alignment probes. The argument does \emph{not} extend to scale: the final Frobenius norm separates E5 from E5c (\Cref{app:frob}) and is informative at step 300 (AUC $0.86$, \Cref{tab:probe}); what it lacks is a threshold that transfers across languages (\Cref{sec:frobenius}). That is why the diagnostic we propose next is a trajectory statistic of scale, not a property of the final direction. Llama-3 satisfies the same inequality (within $0.072$ vs.\ between $0.029$; \Cref{app:llama3}), and the argument extends to transfer once the source is reseeded (\Cref{sec:directional}, D2).

\textbf{What generalises, and how far.} \emph{(1) The SE-threshold falsification is existential} and travels furthest: it needs reproduced counterexamples, not coverage, and we give four sub-threshold $r{=}64$ runs spanning the full functional range, the collapse case at three seeds on Gemma and reproduced on Llama-3. We do not claim spectral quantities are uninformative in general. \emph{(2) The underdetermination inequality is a measured regime property} of direct LoRA on two architectures; settings with more canonical solutions (much larger data, different initialisation, shared-source transfer) need not satisfy it. \emph{(3) Frobenius growth and (4) related-language retention are comparative claims} on three Turkic languages plus English; retention reproduces across architectures and under independent reseeding, but three languages cannot establish a general law. The recommendations below follow from (1) and (2).

\subsection{A Trajectory Probe That Does Separate Them}
\label{sec:trajectory_probe}

\Cref{sec:underdetermination} predicts where a working diagnostic must live: not in the final adapter, but in the \emph{trajectory}. We test that prediction directly. Define the mean early growth rate of the update magnitude,
\begin{equation}
g \;=\; \frac{\overline{\|B\|_F}(t_1) - \overline{\|B\|_F}(t_0)}{t_1 - t_0},
\label{eq:growth}
\end{equation}
averaged over all LoRA modules, with $(t_0,t_1) = (100,300)$ training steps---roughly the first $10\%$ of a 3-epoch run.

Across all 42 runs with logged SVD trajectories and a functional label, outside the held-out set below (36 healthy, 6 collapsed, both architectures; every run is listed in \Cref{app:probe_sample}), $g$ separates the classes completely: healthy $\leq 0.0055$, collapsed $\geq 0.0076$. The collapsed minimum is E7, a run at $r{=}64$, $\text{lr}{=}10^{-3}$ whose target-language perplexity rose from $17$ to $870$ between steps 400 and 600 and which was stopped at step 850 without downstream evaluation; $g$ flags it at step 300, before the divergence, and without it the gap is $2.0\times$. The margin widens with the window ($1.4\times$ at $t_1{=}300$, $2.1\times$ at $500$) and holds per architecture (Gemma $1.4\times$, Llama-3 $2.1\times$). It splits the pair that defeats every final-adapter metric we test: E5 and E5c differ $2.3\times$ in $g$ while their spectra are near-identical and their cosine ($0.010$) sits inside the seed-noise band.

Quantitatively (\Cref{tab:probe}), $g$ achieves AUC $1.000$ over the 42 runs, against $0.861$ for the absolute norm at the same checkpoint and $0.051$ for spectral energy: SE is not merely uninformative but \emph{anti}-correlated, because $\text{SE} \approx 1/r$ at initialisation makes every $r{=}64$ run---the family containing all six collapses---look spectrally healthier than the $r{=}16$ baselines throughout training.

Three properties make this usable: it is \textbf{early} (step 300 of 2931, under a tenth of the compute), \textbf{nearly free} ($\|B\|_F$ needs no SVD, no held-out data and no evaluation suite), and \textbf{not a shape metric} (SE at the same checkpoint overlaps between classes and never approaches $0.7$; $g$ uses the scale SE discards).

\textbf{Held-out tests.} The threshold is read off the same 42 runs it separates, so we tested it prospectively on four configurations absent from them, each chosen to break a regularity of the sample: \textbf{H1}, Kyrgyz at $r{=}16$, $\text{lr}{=}10^{-3}$ (every sampled collapse is at $r{=}64$, every $r{=}16$ run healthy); \textbf{H2}, Kyrgyz at $r{=}32$, $\text{lr}{=}5{\times}10^{-4}$ (a rank the sweep lacks); \textbf{H3}, Kyrgyz at $r{=}64$, $\text{lr}{=}3.5{\times}10^{-4}$ (between the healthy $2{\times}10^{-4}$ and collapsed $5{\times}10^{-4}$); and \textbf{H4}, small-corpus Kazakh (the E1b corpus) at $r{=}64$, $\text{lr}{=}5{\times}10^{-4}$ (a language with no sampled collapse). Each verdict was written to disk at step 300, while training continued and before any evaluation (\Cref{tab:probe}). All four read above threshold ($2.6\times$, $1.3\times$, $1.3\times$, $1.8\times$) and were predicted to collapse; all four did: TUMLU at chance in every case ($21$--$26\%$ against $33$--$38\%$ for the baselines), generation NER F1 $0.02$--$0.10$, cross-lingual perplexity up by an order of magnitude (KZ/UZ $160/331$, $370/449$, $401/435$; KY/UZ $218/473$ for H4). H2 and H3, the closest calls, show the E5 signature in full: target Kyrgyz perplexity within $0.1$ of or below the baseline ($4.86$, $3.99$ vs.\ $4.78$), entity-type knowledge intact (H3 TypeAcc $0.63$), yet multiple-choice and structured output at chance. The collapse is invisible to the training loss, to target perplexity and to log-likelihood probes of stored knowledge, and visible in $g$ at a tenth of training; final max SE stays far below $0.7$.

\textbf{Scope.} Four prospective confirmations are not a validated threshold: every held-out run collapsed, so they test sensitivity, and the healthy side rests on the sample itself; a deployable cutoff would need held-out healthy runs near the boundary. The claim is narrower than a threshold: the signal that no direction-based statistic of the final adapter carries is present in the trajectory, cheaply and early.

\textbf{Recommendations.} (i) Do not gate fine-tuning health on SE thresholds; (ii) log $\|B\|_F$ every hundred steps and watch its early growth rate, paired with cross-lingual PPL probes; (iii) treat collapse detection as a functional or trajectory test---no scale-invariant statistic of the final adapter can substitute, and the final norm has no portable threshold; (iv) use related-language transfer initialisation when available; it preserves source-language knowledge, though directional reproducibility requires a shared source (mechanistic context in \Cref{app:mechanistic}).

\section{Conclusion}

We studied LoRA spectral and directional dynamics for three low-resource Turkic languages, replicated across two architectures. \textbf{(1)} The spectral-energy threshold is not predictive: sub-threshold runs span the full range from catastrophic collapse to healthy baseline; the collapse is driven by learning rate, the apparent benefit of higher rank is an artefact of the $\alpha{=}2r$ convention, and a full-precision control rules out quantization. \textbf{(2)} Frobenius growth separates regimes within a language category but not across. \textbf{(3)} The final adapter is structurally underdetermined: within the measured regime, cross-seed directional spread leaves no leverage for any scale-invariant function of the final adapter, and the result is constructive---the early $\|B\|_F$ growth rate separates every collapsed run from every healthy one at a tenth of the training budget, and called four held-out runs correctly before they were evaluated. \textbf{(4)} Related-language warm-start preserves source-language knowledge on both architectures while a cross-family warm-start does not; transfer pins the adapter to its source. For practitioners the consequence is not specific to Turkic: a sub-threshold spectral energy is not evidence of health, no direction-based substitute computed from the final adapter will serve, but the trajectory of its scale will. Future work: held-out healthy runs near the probe's boundary.

\section*{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Our study has several important limitations, which we discuss here in decreasing order of severity for downstream interpretation.

\textbf{(L1) Limited-seed experiments.} E3, E5 and E5c---the configurations carrying the underdetermination evidence---are at $n{=}3$ (seeds 42, 123, 7); the remaining Gemma-2 configurations are at $n{=}2$ except the absolute-$\alpha$ control C5, which is single-seed on Gemma-2 (two-seed on Llama-3); all five Llama-3 configurations are at $n{=}2$. Six further claims hold across both seeds, including the $2\times$ KZ-PPL transfer retention and the EN-vs-Turkic Frobenius dissociation (\Cref{app:seed_variance}). The L3-C5 replication also shows the inequality is not universal across configuration \emph{pairs} ($\cos$(C5-s42,~C5-s123)$=0.200 < \cos$(C5,~E3)$=0.343$): it holds for the collapse-vs-healthy comparisons the argument rests on, and we claim no more. A second Gemma-2 seed for C5 and $n{=}3$ for the transfer configurations would tighten the remaining comparisons.

\textbf{(L2) Training-set contamination for NER (quantified).} Our Turkic corpora include Wikipedia, and WikiANN derives from Wikipedia entity links \cite{pan2017cross}. Verbatim overlap between gold test entities and each training corpus is $65.7\%$ (KZ), $55.9\%$ (KY) and $2.2\%$ (UZ), so KZ/KY NER numbers upper-bound memorization at ${\sim}60\%$ and cross-configuration comparisons within those languages are partially confounded. The UZ figure is a script-mismatch artifact (L9) that incidentally makes Uzbek a memorization-clean probe. A non-Wikipedia benchmark such as KazNERD \cite{yeshpanov2022kaznerd} would tighten this; per-type breakdown in \Cref{app:overlap}.

\textbf{(L3) Rank--LR--epoch confound (closed by E5b, E5c, C5).} E5 varied four factors at once; E5b matches rank at baseline LR, E5c additionally matches epochs, and C5 additionally matches absolute $\alpha$. Together they decompose it: catastrophic collapse $=$ aggressive LR; extra cross-lingual forgetting $=$ training duration; apparent rank-only advantage $=$ the $\alpha{=}2r$ convention amplifying absolute $\alpha$, refuted by C5 (\Cref{sec:spectral-collapse-rank-lr}). Rank itself, at matched $\alpha$, leaves only a small target-PPL gain and no cross-lingual or task benefit.

\textbf{(L4) Quantization confound (addressed by C3).} The BF16 control (Kyrgyz, $r{=}16$, identical to E3 but full-precision) refutes the concern that 4-bit NF4 suppresses singular-value growth: SE is statistically indistinguishable from 4-bit E3 at every comparable point, and Frobenius, PPL, NER and TUMLU fall within seed variance (\Cref{app:bf16}). C3 is at two seeds on Gemma-2; the full-precision control is not replicated on Llama-3.

\textbf{(L5) Corpus imbalance (partially addressed).} Corpora are byte-matched (${\sim}150$~MB) but token counts differ (KZ 14.1M, UZ 5.4M, KY 4.4M) because records are truncated to 256 tokens and the Kyrgyz and Uzbek records are long (\Cref{sec:data}); each language trains on a different fraction of its corpus. The small-corpus KZ control E1b (${\sim}1.5$M tokens) separates volume from coverage: KZ PPL rises $2.73\to4.17$, so volume matters, yet $4.17$ still beats the KY baseline's $4.78$ at ${\sim}3\times$ fewer tokens, so Kazakh also enjoys a tokenizer/pretraining-coverage advantage. Both contribute.

\textbf{(L6) NER as a primary metric.} Generation-based NER with 3-shot prompting at $n{=}100$ is closer to a test of instruction-following than of entity knowledge, and the $r{=}64$ ``F1${=}0$'' is driven by parse failures. This is why log-likelihood span typing (\Cref{app:ner_loglik}) is our primary NER metric; it shows entity knowledge survives the collapse that generation F1 reports as total.

\textbf{(L7) Scope of generalization.} The Gemma sweep is replicated on Llama-3-8B across five configurations and every pattern holds, though absolute cross-seed cosines run ${\sim}2\times$ higher there: the scale of seed-orthogonality is architecture-dependent, the regime structure is not (\Cref{app:llama3}). Our sample is three of ${\sim}35$ Turkic languages. The single-family design is deliberate: H2 asks whether \emph{relatedness} preserves source knowledge, which requires holding family fixed and varying it only in the EN$\rightarrow$KY control---a cross-family sweep would confound relatedness with script, morphology and pretraining coverage at once. The cost is that our findings are established \emph{for} agglutinative, Cyrillic-script, severely underrepresented languages; transfer to other low-resource settings is untested and we scope the claims accordingly.

\textbf{(L8/L9)} Two further limitations are detailed in \Cref{app:add_limitations}: the Frobenius growth ranges of \Cref{sec:frobenius} are observations on the runs their interpretation is built on, not held-out thresholds; and our Cyrillic-only Uzbek corpus against Latin-script WikiANN-uz explains the $2\%$ entity overlap of L2, limiting UZ generalisability while making it a memorization-clean transfer probe.

\section*{Ethical considerations}

We fine-tune language models on corpora from three Turkic languages and English. While we aim to improve NLP for underserved language communities, models can amplify biases in their training data; our corpora, drawn from Wikipedia, literary texts and web sources, may carry cultural bias or factual error.

\textbf{Data availability:} The Kazakh and Uzbek corpora are compiled from publicly available sources (Wikipedia, open book collections). The Kyrgyz corpus is curated from licensed literary and encyclopedic sources and cannot be redistributed due to copyright restrictions. In the spirit of open science, we release all experimental code, SVD monitoring logs, and evaluation results via an anonymous repository (see Reproducibility Statement). Trained LoRA adapters will be publicly released upon acceptance.

\section{Effective Rank Dynamics}
\label{app:effrank}

\Cref{fig:effrank} shows the effective rank dynamics across experiments. The $r{=}64$ configuration shows increasing effective rank (from $\sim$6 to $\sim$26), indicating growing parameter utilization. The diverged experiment (E7) is the only configuration where effective rank \emph{decreases} (from 43.3 to 37.1), consistent with true spectral collapse.

\textbf{E7 divergence analysis.} The diverged experiment ($r{=}64$, $\text{lr}{=}10^{-3}$) provides the most extreme datapoint. Training diverged between steps 400 and 600 and was stopped at step 850; at the last SVD log (step 800), maximum SE was only 0.18---still far below the 0.7 threshold---while mean Frobenius norm had already reached 6.75 (19$\times$ growth in just 800 steps, compared to 15.6$\times$ over 4,885 steps for E5). The effective rank decrease (43.3 $\rightarrow$ 37.1) in the final 200 steps is the only instance of true rank contraction in our experiments, suggesting that SE-based collapse detection may only trigger \emph{after} the model has already diverged---too late for practical intervention. The Frobenius norm, by contrast, shows clear exponential growth from step 100 onward, providing a much earlier warning signal.

\section{Log-Likelihood NER: Type Classification under Gold Spans}
\label{app:ner_loglik}

\section{Additional Limitations (L8, L9)}
\label{app:add_limitations}

\textbf{(L8) Post-hoc Frobenius observations.} The growth ranges in \Cref{sec:frobenius} are derived from the same experiments on which their interpretation is built; they are observations requiring held-out validation, not established thresholds, and we deliberately reframe them as such in \Cref{sec:frobenius}.

\textbf{(L9) Uzbek script coverage.} Our Uzbek training corpus is restricted to Cyrillic-script sources, while WikiANN-uz uses primarily Latin script: this is the most likely explanation for the $2\%$ entity-string overlap reported in L2 (vs.\ $\sim 60\%$ for KZ/KY where script alignment is uniform Cyrillic). Since Uzbekistan officially uses Latin script and most contemporary formal text is Latin-based, our Uzbek perplexity and target-language NER results should not be generalized to Latin-script data without a separate evaluation. The script-mismatch nonetheless gives us an unintended benefit: Uzbek serves as a cleaner cross-lingual probe than KZ or KY because memorization-driven inflation is negligible, so cross-lingual UZ NER scores are difficult to attribute to data leakage.

\section{Why Spectral Energy Fails}
\label{app:why_se_fails}

Standard LoRA initialises $A \sim$ Kaiming, $B{=}0$, so after the first update $B$ has near-uniform small singular values and $\text{SE} \approx 1/r$. Training grows them jointly across many directions, so SE stays flat or falls. Two consequences follow. It is \emph{rank-biased}: $1/64 \approx 0.016$ versus $1/16 = 0.063$, a $3$--$4\times$ gap that has nothing to do with functional outcome---higher-rank adapters look spectrally healthier by construction, which is exactly the inversion \Cref{tab:probe} quantifies. And it is a \emph{shape} metric, invariant to rescaling: the spectra $[100,50,25]$ and $[10^{-3}, 5{\times}10^{-4}, 2.5{\times}10^{-4}]$ have identical SE despite perturbation magnitudes five orders of magnitude apart. The scale information SE discards is precisely what separates collapse from health here.

\section{Mechanistic Context}
\label{app:mechanistic}

Three lines of prior work connect to our findings.

\textbf{Loss-surface geometry and mode connectivity.} \citet{garipov2018loss} and \citet{frankle2020linear} showed that independently trained networks often occupy distinct minima connected only by non-trivial paths, and that linear mode connectivity emerges only after a stability point in training. Our cross-seed cosines for direct LoRA fine-tuning ($\sim 0.02$--$0.04$) are the LoRA-update analogue: two seeds of an identical configuration end up in essentially orthogonal regions of $BA$ space, indicating that direct adapter optimisation does not enter a single shared basin. Transfer initialisation \emph{from a shared source} (E6 with both seeds warm-started from E1-s42: $\cos = 0.785$, $294/294 > 0.5$) is the linearly-connected case in this analogy: starting from a non-trivial pinning point produces a single shared cone whose endpoint is reproducible. \emph{Without} a shared source (independent E1-s42 and E1-s123: $\cos = 0.023$), the connected-cone property does not propagate -- the source itself behaves like an independently-trained network.

\textbf{Intrinsic dimensionality.} \citet{aghajanyan2021intrinsic} showed that LLM fine-tuning operates in a very low intrinsic dimension---a small number of directions suffice to recover most of the task gain. A natural reading of our directional non-canonicity is that the low intrinsic dimension is a property of the \emph{loss landscape}, not of any specific axis: there are many low-dimensional subspaces that produce equivalent target-language fit, and direct LoRA selects among them stochastically through seed/initialisation. \emph{Which} low-dim subspace is selected varies across seeds; the shape (singular-value distribution) is rank-biased and similar across runs, the spatial orientation is not.

\textbf{LP-FT and feature distortion.} \citet{kumar2022finetuning} argued that full fine-tuning can distort pretrained features in ways that hurt OOD behaviour, and that linear-probe-then-fine-tune (LP-FT) mitigates this. Our cross-lingual forgetting under direct LoRA training is consistent with feature distortion: large $\|B\|_F$ growth on a Turkic adapter induces high cross-lingual PPL on other languages despite sub-threshold SE. Transfer-init functions as an LP-FT-like prior for the low-resource cross-lingual setting: a warm-start adapter has already paid most of the distortion cost on a related task, so the residual fine-tuning lives in a narrower cone and disturbs fewer pretrained directions. The graded E6 vs.\ E6b contrast (related-language vs.\ cross-family warm-start) shows the regularising effect scales with source-target similarity.

\textbf{LoRA initialisation work.} PiSSA \citep{meng2024pissa} initialises LoRA factors from a truncated SVD of $W_0$, biasing the adapter toward already-important pretrained directions; LoRA+ \citep{hayou2024loraplus} adjusts learning rates of $A$ vs.\ $B$ to address LoRA's factorisation asymmetry. Both are direction-pinning interventions in our sense: they constrain where the optimisation can start (PiSSA) or how it can move (LoRA+). Our directional analysis suggests an empirical question for that line of work: \emph{because PiSSA initialises both seeds from the same SVD-derived point} (the $W_0$ truncation is deterministic), it should sit in our shared-init regime; do the resulting cross-seed cosines reach E6's shared-init level ($\sim 0.78$), or only the partial regime of E6b ($\sim 0.34$)? Independence-fair cross-seed protocols (different deterministic init schemes per seed) would be needed to test PiSSA against the independent-init regime ($\sim 0.02$). The per-module pairwise-cosine protocol of \Cref{sec:directional} provides a direct test.

The empirical structural claim we add to this body of work is that within standard direct LoRA fine-tuning the directional non-canonicity is large enough to set the noise floor on any final-adapter-geometric diagnostic, and that the connection between this noise floor and cross-lingual catastrophic-forgetting phenomenology is, to our knowledge, novel.

\section{Frobenius Growth: Full Discussion}
\label{app:frobenius}

The Frobenius growth ratio $\|B\|_F^{\text{final}}/\|B\|_F^{\text{init}}$ captures scale information SE discards, and separates regimes within an underrepresented-language category ($1.1\times$ transfer, $8.3$--$8.7\times$ direct KY at either rank, $15.6$--$18.2\times$ for the $r{=}64$ runs that forget severely; \Cref{tab:frobenius}, \Cref{app:frob}). It fails across categories: the EN control (E8) has $33\times$ growth---comparable to the KZ baseline's $38\times$---but minimal cross-lingual forgetting (KZ PPL $5.52 \pm 0.02$ across 2 seeds vs.\ $\sim 48$ for the KY baseline at $8\times$). E5 and E5b show comparable Frobenius growth ($15.6\times$ vs.\ $18.2\times$), yet only E5 produces NER parse failure. Norm growth is a reliable diagnostic \emph{within} a language category but not \emph{across}: the same magnitude of perturbation has qualitatively different effects depending on target-language representedness in the base. We take up the directional question in \Cref{sec:directional}.

\section{Cross-Lingual Transfer: Full Results}
\label{app:transfer}

KZ$\rightarrow$KY transfer (E6) initializes LoRA from the final Kazakh adapter (E1) before continuing on Kyrgyz. The robust effect (both seeds 42 and 123) is \emph{source-language retention}: cross-lingual KZ PPL $23.5 \pm 0.1$ under E6 vs.\ $50.0 \pm 2.4$ under direct KY training (E3)---both reported as two-seed mean and half-range; the single-seed-42 numbers in \Cref{tab:cross_ppl} ($23.49$ vs.\ $47.58$) sit inside these ranges---a $2\times$ improvement well outside seed variance, achieved with only $+13\%$ Frobenius growth vs.\ $8.4\times$ for direct training. \subsection*{Cosine computation}
\label{app:cosine_details}
\Cref{eq:cosine} is evaluated without forming the $\text{out}\times\text{in}$ product, via $\langle B_1 A_1, B_2 A_2\rangle_F = \mathrm{tr}(A_1^{\top} B_1^{\top} B_2 A_2)$, which needs only $r \times r$ intermediates.

\textbf{$\alpha$ matters for direction more than rank does (C5 control).} At identical $r/\text{lr}/\text{epochs}/\text{seed}/\text{data}$, $\cos$(E5c, C5)$=0.072$, near the cross-seed band, but $\cos$(C5, E3)$=0.155$ ($2/294$ modules above $0.5$)---higher than any other direct-from-base pair. So $r{=}64$ at $\alpha{=}32$ (C5) sits directionally closer to the $r{=}16$ baseline (also $\alpha{=}32$) than to the $r{=}64$ E5c (which uses $\alpha{=}128$). Consistent with the functional finding (\Cref{sec:spectral-collapse-rank-lr}): $\alpha$---via its role in effective per-step LR---is the variable that determines where the adapter lands.

\textbf{Robustness.} \texttt{final\_adapter/} is the best-eval-loss checkpoint (HF \texttt{load\_best\_model\_at\_end=True}); re-running the eleven principal pairs against the literal last saved checkpoint changes each cosine by at most $\pm 0.006$ (\Cref{app:lastckpt_robustness}). Seed-orthogonality is stable across training: cross-seed direct cosines stay in $0.02$--$0.04$ from the earliest checkpoint through best-eval, and E6 stays at $\sim 0.78$ throughout (\Cref{app:trajectory}).

\textbf{Llama-3-8B replication.} The L3 sweep (E3, E5c, E5 seed-replicated at $n{=}2$; plus the C5 absolute-$\alpha$ control and a fully-independent two-seed E6 KZ$\rightarrow$KY transfer pipeline) reproduces all four paper-level patterns: max SE $\leq 0.34$, L3-E5 NER F1$=0$ with TypeAcc preserved at $0.65$--$0.80$, the rank-only E5c effect and its cross-lingual cost reproduce, and the underdetermination inequality holds---$\cos$(L3-E5c-s42, L3-E5c-s123)$=0.072$ (within-config) $>$ $\cos$(L3-E5, L3-E5c)$=0.029$ (between-config), mirroring Gemma's $0.032 > 0.010$. Source-init pinning under transfer also replicates: $\cos$(L3-E6, L3-KZ-source)$=0.804$, mirroring Gemma's $0.797$. The independent two-source transfer cross-seed on Llama-3 gives $\cos$(L3-E6-s42, L3-E6-s123)$=0.060$ -- the architecturally-fair counterpart of the Gemma $0.023$ test (\Cref{tab:pairwise_cos}, \Cref{app:llama3}).

\section{Llama-3-8B Replication}
\label{app:llama3}

We replicate five of the critical Gemma-2-9B configurations on Llama-3-8B \citep{dubey2024llama3} via the byte-identical NousResearch mirror of the gated Meta repository. Same data, same hyperparameters as the Gemma originals, same 4-bit NF4 + QLoRA setup, seeds 42 and 123 for all five configurations (L3-E6 with an independently re-seeded source). Runs: \textbf{L3-E3} ($r{=}16$, $\alpha{=}32$, $\text{lr}{=}2{\times}10^{-4}$, 3 epochs); \textbf{L3-E5c} ($r{=}64$, $\alpha{=}128$, $\text{lr}{=}2{\times}10^{-4}$, 3 epochs); \textbf{L3-E5} ($r{=}64$, $\alpha{=}128$, $\text{lr}{=}5{\times}10^{-4}$, 5 epochs); \textbf{L3-C5} ($r{=}64$, absolute $\alpha{=}32$ -- breaking the $\alpha{=}2r$ convention -- $\text{lr}{=}2{\times}10^{-4}$, 3 epochs); and a two-stage transfer pipeline \textbf{L3-E6} (Kazakh pretrain at $r{=}16$, $\alpha{=}32$ on the 14.1M KZ corpus, then KZ$\rightarrow$KY warm-start on the 4.4M KY corpus at the same hyperparameters).

\textbf{All central claims of the paper replicate qualitatively} (\Cref{tab:llama3}). \textbf{(i)} SE never reaches the $0.7$ threshold (max SE: L3-E3 $0.335$, L3-E5c $0.137$, L3-E5 $0.138$; cf.\ Gemma-2 $0.322$, $0.112$, $0.058$). \textbf{(ii)} L3-E5 exhibits the same functional-vs-spectral collapse dissociation: NER F1 (gen)~$=0$ on all three languages (parse failure rates $97/94/71\%$ across KY/KZ/UZ), TUMLU drops to $24.7\%$ (near $25\%$ random), \emph{but} log-likelihood NER TypeAcc is preserved or even higher than the baseline ($0.649/0.741/0.796$ vs.\ L3-E3 $0.577/0.696/0.680$). Entity knowledge survives; structured-output instruction-following collapses; SE does not see it. \textbf{(iii)} Cross-lingual forgetting in L3-E5 is severe (KZ PPL $145$, UZ PPL $286$ vs.\ L3-E3 $30/54$), in the same direction as Gemma though milder in absolute magnitude. \textbf{(iv)} The apparent rank-only target-language improvement of E5c over E3 reproduces on Llama-3 ($3.63$ vs.\ $4.32$ KY PPL; $3.86$ vs.\ $4.78$ on Gemma) with the same cross-lingual cost (KZ $41$ vs.\ $30$ on Llama-3). \textbf{(v) The L3-C5 control closes the absolute-$\alpha$ story on Llama-3:} holding $\alpha{=}32$ at $r{=}64$ brings the adapter close to the $r{=}16$ baseline (L3-C5 KY PPL $4.21$ vs.\ L3-E3 $4.32$, against L3-E5c's $3.63$; KZ PPL $29.9$ vs.\ $30.4$; UZ PPL $51.5$ vs.\ $54.3$), while L3-E5c's apparent target-PPL gain ($3.63$) is bought at the cost of cross-lingual KZ PPL ($41.1$). The cost tracks absolute $\alpha$, not rank---identical to the Gemma C5 result.

\textbf{Directional underdetermination replicates, with quantitative scope.} The critical pairwise cosines on Llama-3 (\Cref{tab:llama3_cos}) preserve the qualitative regime structure of Gemma-2 but at slightly higher absolute values. Cross-configuration: $\cos$(L3-E5, L3-E5c)$=0.029$, $\cos$(L3-E5, L3-E3)$=0.015$, $\cos$(L3-E3, L3-E5c)$=0.113$. Cross-seed: $\cos$(L3-E3-s42, L3-E3-s123)$=0.103$, $\cos$(L3-E5c-s42, L3-E5c-s123)$=0.072$. The Llama-3 cross-seed cosines are roughly $2\times$ higher than the Gemma counterparts ($0.03$--$0.04$), so direct LoRA training on Llama-3 is somewhat less seed-orthogonal than on Gemma. The underdetermination inequality of \Cref{sec:underdetermination} is satisfied on this architecture too---the within-configuration cross-seed cosine ($0.072$ for E5c) is \emph{larger} than the between-configuration cosine between collapse and healthy at the same rank ($0.029$ for E5 vs E5c), so no direction-based statistic gains leverage from the geometry, exactly as on Gemma. The C5 directional probe replicates: $\cos$(L3-C5, L3-E3) $=0.343$ (same absolute $\alpha$, different rank) vs.\ $\cos$(L3-E5c, L3-C5) $=0.162$ (same rank, $4\times$ absolute $\alpha$) -- at matched absolute $\alpha$ the adapter occupies a near-shared direction regardless of rank, mirroring Gemma's $0.155$ vs.\ $0.072$ contrast at $\sim 2\times$ scale. Seed-replicating C5 brings Llama-3 to $n{=}2$ on all five configurations and exposes a boundary of the inequality worth stating plainly: $\cos$(C5-s42, C5-s123)$=0.200$ is \emph{below} the between-configuration $\cos$(C5, E3)$=0.343$. For this pair the within-config spread does \emph{not} exceed the between-config gap, so the inequality of \Cref{sec:underdetermination} does not hold universally across configuration pairs---it holds for the collapse-vs-healthy comparisons the argument is about, on both architectures. The C5/E3 exception is itself consistent with the mechanism we report: two runs sharing an absolute $\alpha$ land in a common direction \emph{across ranks} more reliably than two seeds of one configuration do, which is the strongest form of the ``$\alpha$ determines direction, rank does not'' finding. Functionally C5 reproduces tightly across seeds (target KY PPL $4.21$ vs.\ $4.19$; TypeAcc $0.568$ vs.\ $0.577$), the same behaviour-vs-direction dissociation seen throughout.

\textbf{Source-init pinning is architecture-independent; canonical convergence is not.} The L3-E6 two-stage pipeline at seed 42 trains a Kazakh source-language baseline (L3-KZ-s42, KZ eval PPL $2.76$) and then warm-starts on Kyrgyz under the same hyperparameters as L3-E3. Functionally, L3-E6 \textbf{retains Kazakh under Kyrgyz training}: KZ PPL drops from $30.38$ (L3-E3 direct) to $19.04$ (L3-E6 transfer), a $37\%$ reduction (Gemma E6: $\sim 53\%$); target KY PPL is comparable ($4.32 \to 4.12$); UZ degrades ($54.3 \to 81.6$), consistent with the transfer originating from KZ rather than UZ. Directionally, L3-E6 is pinned to its source: $\cos$(L3-E6-s42, L3-KZ-s42-source) $= 0.804$ with $224/224$ modules above $0.5$, mirroring Gemma E6-s42 vs.\ E1-s42 source ($0.797$). \textbf{However, two transfers from \emph{independent} sources do \emph{not} share direction across seeds:} we ran the architecturally-fair test on both architectures -- L3-E6-s42-from-L3-KZ-s42 vs.\ L3-E6-s123-from-L3-KZ-s123 gives $\cos = 0.060$ ($1/224$ above $0.5$); the Gemma counterpart (E6-s42-from-E1-s42 vs.\ E6-indep-s123-from-E1-s123) gives $\cos = 0.023$ ($0/294$). Both numbers sit inside the direct-training cross-seed band of \Cref{sec:directional}. The Gemma cross-seed cosine of $0.785$ in \Cref{tab:pairwise_cos} reflects two stage-2 warm-starts from a \emph{shared} E1-s42 source; it is essentially a measure of source-init pinning, not cross-seed canonicality. \emph{Functionally}, transfer retention reproduces across architectures and across independent reseeding (L3-E6-s123 from L3-KZ-s123: KY $4.17$, KZ $18.5$; Gemma E6-indep-s123 from E1-s123: KY $4.58$, \textbf{KZ $26.79$ vs direct training $50.0\pm 2.4$, a $-47\%$ retention effect that matches the original shared-init E6 within seed spread}). \emph{Directionally}, transfer pins to its source but does not produce a canonical direction -- the underdetermination evidence of \Cref{sec:underdetermination} extends to transfer once the source is reseeded.

\textbf{Functional consistency across L3 seeds.} \Cref{tab:llama3_seed} shows that the directional spread across seeds is not matched by a similar spread in downstream functional metrics: target PPL stays within $\pm 0.05$ across seeds for both E3 and E5c, TUMLU within $\pm 1.5$ percentage points, and TypeAcc within $\pm 0.03$. This is the same behaviour-vs-direction dissociation we observe on Gemma: two seeds of the same configuration produce essentially identical model behaviour through essentially orthogonal weight-space directions.
