\section*{Overview}
The May 2026 reviews rated the study sound (Soundness 4.5 / 4 / 3) and the
meta-review recommended Findings; the principal objections were (i) a
citation error, (ii) limited statistical strength ($n{=}2$), (iii) Llama-3
controls missing for transfer and the absolute-$\alpha$ control, (iv) an
overstated Lipschitz claim and title, and (v) dense presentation without a
stated research question. Every one of these is addressed in this version,
and the paper now adds a positive result the previous version lacked: a
trajectory diagnostic that separates every collapsed run from every healthy
one at a tenth of the training budget
(\S4.9)\rev{, validated on held-out runs whose outcome was predicted before
evaluation (\S4.9, Table~X)}.\footnote{\rev{Placeholder until the held-out
runs finish; delete this sentence if they are not included.}}

\section*{1. Citation integrity (meta-review)}
The meta-review correctly identified that the TUMLU reference in the
submitted version was wrong: it listed ``Maral Isbarov and others, EMNLP
2024''. The benchmark we used is
\emph{TUMLU: A Unified and Native Language Understanding Benchmark for Turkic
Languages}, Jafar Isbarov et al., ACL 2025 (Long Papers),
\url{https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1112/}. The entry was a
placeholder created when the benchmark was still a preprint and was never
replaced; we apologise for the error. The reference is corrected, and
\emph{every} entry in the bibliography has since been checked against the
ACL Anthology, arXiv, or the publisher's page (author list, title, venue,
year, identifier). \rev{[List any further corrections found by the check

\section*{2. Point-by-point responses}

\subsection*{Area Chair (QZhf)}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Presentation: simplify the abstract, fewer experiment IDs and
numbers in the main text.} The abstract is rewritten around four findings
with no experiment IDs; the introduction now opens with the practical
problem and states the research question explicitly (\S1, second
paragraph); experiment IDs in the body are retained only where a table row
is being referenced.
\item \textbf{Scope of the SE result and generalisation.} \S4.8 now carries a
paragraph ``What generalises, and how far'' that assigns a scope to each of
the four claims: the SE falsification is existential (reproduced
counterexamples on two architectures, three seeds); the underdetermination
inequality is a measured regime property, not a law; Frobenius growth and
related-language retention are comparative claims on three languages. The
conclusion repeats this scoping.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Reviewer WF36 (Overall 4, Soundness 4.5, Excitement 3.5)}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Abstract too dense.} Rewritten (see above).
\item \textbf{Warm-start result only on Gemma-2.} Replicated on Llama-3-8B:
KZ$\to$KY transfer halves cross-lingual KZ perplexity there as well
($30.4\to19.0$; \S4.5, Appendix~V), and the directional source-pinning
(cosine to source init 0.804 on Llama-3 vs.\ 0.785 on Gemma) reproduces.
\item \textbf{Small scale, one or two seeds.} The three configurations carrying
the underdetermination inequality (E3, E5, E5c) are now at $n{=}3$ (seeds
42, 123, 7); the inequality holds for all three cross-seed pairs of each
configuration (\S4.8, Table~3). Eleven of twelve Gemma configurations are
seed-replicated.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Reviewer Fziu (Overall 2, Soundness 3, Excitement 2)}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{``No Lipschitz function'' is too strong; it is an empirical
result, not a proof.} Agreed and revised throughout. The claim is now stated
as a \emph{measured bound}: ``within the seed-noise regime we measure, the
within-configuration spread bounds the SNR of any Lipschitz function of the
final adapter'' (abstract, \S4.8, conclusion), with an explicit sentence
that this is ``an empirical inequality, not a proof of impossibility''
(abstract) and that settings with more canonical solutions ``need not
satisfy it'' (\S4.8, scope paragraph).
\item \textbf{$n{=}2$ gives no variance estimate.} The inequality is now
measured at $n{=}3$ for E3, E5 and E5c: within-configuration spreads
$0.0316\pm0.0002$, $0.0221\pm0.0003$, $0.0423\pm0.0013$ (mean$\pm$SD over the
three cross-seed pairs) against between-configuration gaps of $0.0101$ and
$0.0053$; the smallest within-configuration value exceeds the largest
between-configuration gap by $3$--$8\times$ for every pair (\S4.8).
\item \textbf{Llama-3 replication incomplete (transfer, absolute-$\alpha$,
BF16).} The absolute-$\alpha$ control (L3-C5) and the KZ$\to$KY transfer
(L3-E6) are now replicated on Llama-3-8B, L3-C5 at two seeds and L3-E6 with
an independently re-seeded source (Appendix~V). Every Gemma pattern
reproduces, including the inequality ($0.072>0.029$) and the source-pinning
of transfer. We also report where the inequality does \emph{not} hold
(cos(C5-s42, C5-s123)$=0.200 <$ cos(C5, E3)$=0.343$), and state that the
claim is made only for the collapse-vs-healthy comparisons (Limitations L1).
The BF16 control remains Gemma-only and single-seed; this is stated in L4.
\rev{[If a second BF16 seed is run before submission, update here and L4.]}
\item \textbf{Title and core claim should be narrowed.} The title no longer
asserts a general negative. The SE falsification is stated for ``the
$\mathrm{SE}>0.7$ threshold in low-resource quantized LoRA'' and the general
claim the paper makes is positive: the diagnostic signal lives in the
trajectory (\S4.9).
\item \textbf{Software 1, Datasets 1, Reproducibility 2.} These scores were
assigned on the premise that no code was released. The anonymous repository
cited in footnote~1 (abstract) and in the Reproducibility Statement is live
and contains all training, evaluation and SVD-monitoring code, the SVD logs
and the per-run evaluation reports for every configuration, including the
new seed-7 and Llama-3 runs. Reviewer WF36 scored the same artifacts
Software 4 / Reproducibility 4. We ask that the artifact scores be read
against the repository.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Reviewer ECn6 (Overall 3, Soundness 4, Excitement 2)}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{LoRA not spelled out; $\Delta W$, $A$, $B$, $\sigma$, $\alpha$, $r$
undefined at first use.} The introduction now defines LoRA and every symbol
in its second paragraph before any result is stated.
\item \textbf{Motivation and research question missing.} The introduction opens
with the practical problem (a cheap health check is needed exactly where
evaluation suites do not exist) and states the research question in
italics: \emph{can any function of the final adapter's weights be trusted to
detect fine-tuning failure in this regime?} The three hypotheses are
introduced as the operationalisation of that question.
\item \textbf{Implications for the broader community.} The introduction and
\S4.9 give the recommendations directly: do not gate training health on SE;
log $\|B\|_F$ every hundred steps and watch its early growth rate; treat
collapse detection as a functional test; prefer a related-language
warm-start. The conclusion states these are not specific to Turkic.
\item \textbf{Introduction should abstract away from parameters.} Model and
configuration details are moved to \S3; the introduction now carries only
the argument.
\end{itemize}
